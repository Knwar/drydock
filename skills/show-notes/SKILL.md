# Show Notes and Episode Packaging

Everything around an episode that isn't the recording: the script or outline that shapes it before, and the notes, titles, chapters, and derivatives that carry it after. Works for audio podcasts, video podcasts, and YouTube-style episodes.

## When to use

- Before recording: an episode script, run-of-show, or segment outline.
- After recording: show notes, title options, the description, chapter markers, guest bio, links list, from a transcript (or the outline plus host notes if there is no transcript).
- Repurposing: clip selection, social posts, a newsletter section, a blog recap.

For story structure inside the episode see the podcast-production skill; for interview questions see podcast-interview.

## Core principles

1. **The transcript is the only source of truth.** Every quote, timestamp, name, number, claim, and link in the notes must appear in the transcript or in material the user supplied. If it isn't there, it doesn't go in.
2. **Never fabricate.** No invented quotes, paraphrases presented as quotes, timestamps, links, guest credentials, or book titles. A missing item is marked `[NEEDS: …]` for the user to fill, never guessed. A plausible-looking URL is worse than no URL.
3. **Write for the person deciding whether to listen.** Lead with what they'll get, in their words, not with "In this episode we discuss…".
4. **Timestamps are references, not decoration.** Use them only when the transcript has them; copy them, don't estimate. Note that edits after transcription shift timings.
5. **One episode, many surfaces.** Plan derivatives while you have the transcript open; each is adapted to its channel.

## Method: from transcript to show notes

1. **Check the inputs.** Transcript (with or without timestamps; auto-generated or corrected), episode number and series name, guest names and how they want to be described, links the host promised, sponsor reads, and the destination (podcast app, YouTube, website). Note anything missing.
2. **Read the whole transcript.** Mark: the central question or promise, 4 to 8 key points, the strongest quotable lines (exact wording, with timestamp if present), every resource mentioned (books, tools, people, studies, websites, episodes), and any claim that might need a correction or caveat.
3. **Verify names.** Spell people, companies, and products as in the user's materials; flag auto-transcription guesses (a name spelled two ways, a product name rendered as common words) as `[CHECK SPELLING]`.
4. **Build the links list from mentions only.** For each resource mentioned, list it with what was said about it. Add a URL only if the user supplied it, it's in the transcript, or you have verified it points to that exact resource; otherwise `[NEEDS LINK]`.
5. **Draft the notes** using `references/templates.md`: summary, key points, timestamps or chapters, guest section, links, credits.
6. **Write titles and description** (see below), with options.
7. **Draft chapter markers** if the transcript has timestamps.
8. **Plan derivatives** (clips, posts, newsletter section).
9. **Self-check** against the fabrication checklist before handing over.

## Titles

Write 5 to 10 options, then recommend one with a reason.

- Lead with the specific payoff or the most interesting idea, not the guest's name, unless the guest's name is itself the draw.
- Concrete beats clever: a reader should know what they'll learn.
- Keep it short enough to survive truncation in podcast apps and search results; put the key words first.
- Include the episode number only if the show's convention does, and in the same format.
- No clickbait the episode doesn't pay off; no claims the guest didn't make.
- Mix types: the promise ("How to…"), the surprising claim (only if said in the episode), the question, the guest-plus-topic.

## Description

- First one to two sentences do the work: they show in previews. Say who, what, and why it matters to the listener.
- Then two to four sentences or bullets on what's covered.
- Then guest line, links, sponsor disclosure if any, and the show's standard footer.
- Plain text friendly: many podcast apps strip formatting; YouTube descriptions are plain text with timestamps on their own lines.

## Chapter markers

- Only from real transcript timestamps. If timings come from an unedited recording, say the markers need re-timing after the edit.
- First chapter starts at 00:00. For YouTube chapters the convention is a list starting at 0:00 with at least three chapters, each at least ten seconds long; check the platform's current rules before relying on this.
- 4 to 12 chapters for a typical episode; each a short, specific label of what happens there ("Why the first launch failed"), not "Discussion".
- Chapters mark topic shifts, not every question.

## Episode scripts and outlines

Use for solo, co-hosted, or interview shows. Formats are in `references/templates.md`:

- **Cold open**: 15 to 60 seconds; the best moment, a question, or a tension, before any intro.
- **Intro**: show name, who's speaking, the episode's promise in one sentence; keep it short.
- **Segments**: each with a purpose, key points or questions, a transition line in, and a target time.
- **Mid-roll / sponsor read**: placed at a natural break, disclosed.
- **Outro**: the one takeaway, call to action (one), what's next, credits.

Write for the ear: short sentences, one idea each, no parentheticals, numbers rounded and spoken naturally. Mark which lines are scripted word-for-word and which are talking points.

## Repurposing

From one episode, typically:

- **Clips** (30 to 90 seconds): each a self-contained moment: one idea, a hook in the first seconds, a clear end. Give in/out timestamps and the exact transcript text. Pick moments of story, a strong opinion, a surprising fact, or a useful how-to.
- **Social posts**: one key point per post, written natively for the platform, quoting only exact transcript lines.
- **Newsletter section**: the main idea in a short paragraph, one or two takeaways, the listen link.
- **Blog recap / article**: restructured as an article around the central idea, not a transcript dump; quotes exact.
- **Quote cards**: exact lines, attributed to the right speaker.

## Fabrication checklist

Before handing over, check every item:

- [ ] Every quote is verbatim from the transcript and attributed to the right speaker.
- [ ] Every timestamp is copied from the transcript, not estimated.
- [ ] Every link was supplied, in the transcript, or verified; the rest say `[NEEDS LINK]`.
- [ ] Guest bio uses only facts from the guest's supplied bio or the episode itself.
- [ ] No statistic, book title, or name appears that wasn't said or supplied.
- [ ] Unclear auto-transcribed words are flagged, not guessed.
- [ ] Sponsor mentions are disclosed as such.

## When there is no transcript

Work from the outline, host notes, and anything the user tells you about the recording, and say so at the top of the notes.

- Summary and key points come from the outline and are marked "from the outline; confirm against the recording".
- No quotes, no timestamps, no chapters. Offer to add them once a transcript exists.
- Links only from the user's list.

## Summary and key points: weak vs strong

Weak (fits any episode): "In this episode, Sam and Priya dive deep into pricing, discussing strategies, mistakes, and lessons learned along the way."

Strong (specific, from the transcript): "Priya doubled her studio's day rate in one month and lost two clients. She explains why she'd do it again, how she worded the email, and the one client type she now refuses."

Weak key point: "Pricing psychology." Strong key point: "Quote a project price, not an hourly rate, when the client can't judge your speed (14:20)." The strong version only exists if Priya said it at 14:20.

## Common failure modes

- "In this episode, we dive into…" openers and generic summaries that fit any episode.
- Cleaned-up paraphrases put in quotation marks.
- Timestamps invented for an untimestamped transcript, or left unmarked as pre-edit.
- URLs guessed from a resource's name.
- Guest credentials inflated or copied from an outdated bio.
- Key points that list topics ("We talk about pricing") instead of points ("Raise prices before you add features").
- Clips that need ten minutes of context to make sense.
- The same text pasted to every platform.

## Done means

- Show notes with a specific summary, key points, guest section, links list, and credits.
- 5 to 10 title options with one recommended, and a description whose first lines work alone.
- Chapter markers with real timestamps, or a note that none were possible.
- A derivatives list with exact clip in/out times and texts.
- Every gap marked `[NEEDS: …]`, and the fabrication checklist passes.
